CHAPTER IV
PRESENTATION, ANALYSIS AND INTERPRETATION OF DATA
	In this chapter, the researchers present an in-depth analysis of the study’s results, providing a clear summary of the collected data and its significance. The data are organized in tables for clarity, accompanied by a concise discussion that highlights their relevance and implications. 
Profile of the respondents 
	The demographic profile of respondents includes key information relevant to the implementation and evaluation of the ElderCareAI system. The profile covers the respondents’ age, gender, role in the caregiving environment, and years of experience in providing care. It also identifies the facility where they are currently affiliated, specifically LOVING HOME NURSING CARE and Bleu Heritage Nursing Home, the number of caregivers and elderly individuals in each facility, and their responsibilities in assisting elderly patients with daily activities. Additionally, it gathers information about current caregiving practices, challenges encountered, and the use of existing technologies or tools in elderly care. These details provide essential context for understanding the respondents’ backgrounds, assessing the caregiving environment across the two facilities, and evaluating the potential effectiveness and impact of ElderCareAI.
Respondent’s Residence
Figure 1: Elderly Residence
Figure 2: Caregivers Residence
	The survey data in Figure 1 and Figure 2 shows the distribution of elderly and caregiver respondents according to their respective facilities. Out of the total 42 elderly respondents, 19 (45.2%) are from Bleu Heritage, while 23 (54.8%) are from Loving Home Nursing Care and for the caregivers, which totals 69 respondents, 28 (40.58%) are from Bleu Heritage and 41 (59.42%). This indicates a relatively balanced distribution of elderly and caregiver participants across the two facilities, with Loving Home Nursing Care having a slightly higher number of respondents. Such distribution provides insights into the experiences and needs of elderly individuals from both care facilities. Anhang Price et al. (2022) emphasized that survey administration in healthcare settings can influence response rates and the representativeness of respondents. Their review highlighted the importance of considering different patient groups and care settings when collecting survey data. This supports the study's approach of gathering responses from elderly residents across both facilities to obtain varied perspectives and more meaningful feedback for the development of ElderCareAI.
Demographic Profile of Elderly Patients and Caregivers
Figure 3: Demographic Profile of Elderly Patients
	Figure 3 shows that female elderly patients make up the majority, with 71.4% of the respondents, while male elderly patients account for 28.6%. In terms of age, most elderly patients belong to the 70–79 years old group, followed by those aged 60–69 years old. Only a smaller portion are 80 years old Regarding length of stay, most elderly patients have been staying at the facilities for less than one year, while others have stayed for 1–3 years. Only a few have stayed for 4–6 years. This indicates that the facilities accommodate mostly female elderly patients, particularly those aged 70–79, with many being relatively new residents. These findings highlight the importance of ElderCareAI in maintaining updated patient profiles and helping caregivers understand each patient's background and care needs. Kalideen et al. (2022) emphasized the importance of understanding the demographic and clinical profiles of elderly residents in long-term care facilities. Their study supports the need to collect and maintain demographic information, such as age and gender, to help caregivers understand each resident's background and individual care needs.
Figure 4: Demographic Profile of Caregivers
	Figure 4 presents the demographic profiles of caregivers, including the caregivers' age and work experience, as well as the gender distribution. In terms of caregivers' age, the largest group belongs to the 26–35 years old category, followed by those aged 36–45 years old. Meanwhile, caregivers aged 46 and above make up the smallest group. Regarding the gender of caregivers, females represent the majority, while males account for a smaller portion. In terms of caregiving experience, most caregivers have been working for 1–2 years, followed by those with 3–5 years of experience. Only a few have 6–10 years of experience. These findings show that most caregivers are relatively young adults with 1–2 years of caregiving experience, while the elderly patients are predominantly female. This highlights the importance of ElderCareAI in providing an organized platform that supports caregivers with different levels of experience in managing patient information and daily care responsibilities. Chen et al. (2022) emphasized the importance of understanding the demographic and backgrounds of caregivers in long-term care facilities. Their study supports the collection of information such as caregivers' age, gender, and years of working experience, as these factors help provide a better understanding of the individuals responsible for elderly care. 
Caregiver Workload
Figure 5: Caregiver Workload
	Figure 5 shows that 51 caregivers are responsible for 1–5 elderly patients, while 17 caregivers handle 6–10 patients. Meanwhile, 1 caregiver is assigned to 11–20 elderly patients, indicating that some caregivers may face heavier workloads than others. This suggests that caring for multiple elderly patients can be physically and mentally demanding, particularly when caregivers must attend to different needs, monitor patient conditions, and provide continuous assistance. These findings highlight the challenges caregivers experience in managing their responsibilities and the potential of ElderCareAI to help ease their workload through improved patient monitoring and care management.
	Miyaji et al. (2022) found that caregiving tasks can increase stress levels, especially when caregivers have to manage several responsibilities throughout their shifts. This highlights how difficult it can be to monitor patients, keep track of their individual needs, and ensure that each receives proper attention.
Comfortability with Technology
Figure 6: Caregiver Familiarity Using App for Work
Figure 7: Patiennt Comfortability of Wearing a Smartwatch
	Figure 6 shows that 55.1% of caregivers are familiar with using mobile applications for work, while 23.2% are very familiar. On the other hand, 18.8% are unfamiliar, and 2.9% are very unfamiliar with using mobile applications. This indicates that most caregivers already have some experience using mobile technology in their work. However, some may still experience difficulties navigating or adjusting to new applications, highlighting the importance of making ElderCareAI simple, user-friendly, and easy to understand, even for those with limited experience using mobile apps.
	Figure 7 shows that 45.2% of respondents are comfortable wearing a smartwatch, while 33.3% are very comfortable. Meanwhile, 11.9% gave a neutral response, 7.1% feel uncomfortable, and 2.4% selected the very uncomfortable category. This indicates that most respondents are willing to wear a smartwatch, suggesting that they may be comfortable using wearable technology to support their daily caregiving activities. However, the responses from those who feel uncomfortable also highlight the importance of making the device convenient and comfortable for long hours of use. 
	Choukou et al. (2023) highlighted how digital health technologies can help caregivers manage their responsibilities through tools such as reminder systems, mobile applications, and web-based platforms. These technologies can make it easier to keep track of patients' daily activities, medication schedules, and care needs, reducing the difficulty of managing multiple caregiving tasks.
Challenging Health Indicators to Monitor
Figure 8:Challenging Health Indicators to Monitor
	Figure 8 shows that 53 respondents (76.8%) consider stress level as an important health indicator to monitor, followed by sleep pattern with 43 respondents (62.3%). Meanwhile, 34 respondents (49.3%) selected skin temperature, 23 (33.3%) chose heart rate, and 17 (24.6%) selected blood oxygen level (SpO2). Physical activity and fitness received the lowest response, with only 11 respondents (15.9%). This indicates that most respondents are more interested in monitoring stress levels and sleep patterns, which are important in understanding the overall well-being of elderly patients. These findings highlight the importance of including health monitoring features in ElderCareAI to help caregivers keep track of patients' conditions, recognize possible health concerns, and provide appropriate care when needed.
	Alsadoon et al. (2024) emphasized the importance of wearable sensor technology in monitoring elderly patients' health conditions. Their study highlighted how wearable devices can track vital health indicators and securely send collected data to healthcare providers, making it easier to monitor patients and identify changes in their health. This supports the need for ElderCareAI to include wearable health monitoring features that help caregivers keep track of elderly patients' conditions more efficiently.
Reported Health Conditions of Elderly Patients
Figure 9: Reported Health Conditions of Elderly Patients
	Figure 9 shows that "No known condition" received the highest number of selections, with 15 responses. Among the reported health conditions, heart conditions ranked highest with 5 selections, followed by diabetes, hearing loss, hypertension, and stroke, with 4 selections each. Meanwhile, arthritis, blurred vision, depression, high cholesterol, minor movement difficulty, and vision problems received the fewest selections, with only 1 each. This indicates that although some elderly patients have existing health conditions, others have no reported medical concerns. The variety of conditions highlights the importance of ElderCareAI in helping caregivers monitor different health needs, keep track of patient conditions, and provide appropriate care and attention.
	Cajamarca et al. (2023) explained that having multiple chronic health conditions is common among older adults and can affect both their quality of life and the well-being of their caregivers. Their study also highlighted how technologies such as mobile applications and websites can help monitor and manage different health conditions while providing support to caregivers. This emphasizes the importance of ElderCareAI in keeping patient health records organized and helping caregivers monitor different medical conditions more effectively.
Current Process of the Existing System 
	Based on the Likert scale, respondents evaluated the current methods of managing household energy consumption by indicating whether they Strongly Agree, Agree, are Neutral, Disagree, or Strongly Disagree with each statement describing existing practices and processes.
Table 1
ELDERLY PATIENTS’ PERCEPTION TOWARDS THE CURRENT PROCESS OF THE
EXISTING SYSTEMS
	The analysis of Table 1 shows that respondents generally agree that their health conditions are being monitored by caregivers, with a grand mean of 3.78 (Agree). The highest mean of 4.33 (Strongly Agree) indicates that caregivers regularly check the elderly patients' health. Meanwhile, the lowest mean of 2.95 (Neutral) relates to experiencing delays in receiving medical attention during urgent situations. This suggests that although elderly patients generally receive regular health monitoring, there may still be challenges in ensuring timely medical attention and maintaining continuous monitoring.
	Dollard et al. (2022) highlighted the importance of elderly patients feeling safe and properly monitored under their current care arrangements. It also emphasized that monitoring systems are most useful when caregivers can respond promptly to patients' needs. This supports ElderCareAI's goal of improving patient monitoring through health tracking and timely notifications to caregivers.
Table 2
CAREGIVERS’ PERCEPTION TOWARDS THE CURRENT PROCESS OF THE
EXISTING SYSTEMS
	The analysis of Table 2 shows that caregivers generally agree that there are challenges and difficulties in the current caregiving and monitoring process, with a grand mean of 3.78 (Agree). The highest mean of 4.61 (Strongly Agree) indicates that caregivers often feel overwhelmed by the number of elderly patients they need to monitor simultaneously. Meanwhile, the lowest mean of 3.04 (Neutral) relates to their ability to effectively monitor the health vitals of all elderly patients under their care. This suggests that although caregivers are able to monitor their patients to some extent, handling multiple elderly patients at the same time can be challenging, particularly in terms of tracking health conditions, managing time, and responding to patient needs.
	Similarly, Brown et al. (2022) found that caregivers in residential aged-care facilities frequently experienced demanding workloads due to multitasking and the need to constantly shift their attention between different elderly residents. The study revealed that these responsibilities contributed to increased mental workload and stress among care staff. This supports the findings of the present study, particularly the high mean of 4.61 (Strongly Agree), which indicates that caregivers often feel overwhelmed by the number of elderly patients they need to monitor simultaneously. These findings further emphasize the need for ElderCareAI to provide an organized patient monitoring system that can help caregivers manage their responsibilities and monitor elderly patients more efficiently.
Mechanism or Techniques of the Proposed System 
Respondents rated the mechanisms and techniques of the proposed ElderCareAI system using the same scale, reflecting their level of agreement or disagreement with each feature and its potential effectiveness in improving elderly monitoring and care management.
Table 3
MECHANISM OR TECHNIQUES OF THE PROPOSED SYSTEM (ELDERLY)
	The analysis of table 3 shows that elderly respondents strongly agree with the proposed use of wearable health-monitoring technology, with a grand mean of 4.25 (Strongly Agree). The highest mean of 4.38 (Strongly Agree) indicates that respondents believe technology, such as a health-monitoring smartwatch, can improve their overall safety and well-being. Meanwhile, the lowest mean of 4.14 (Agree) relates to their concerns about wearing a health-monitoring device throughout the day. This suggests that elderly respondents generally have a positive perception of wearable health-monitoring technology and are willing to adopt it as part of their daily care. Their responses also indicate that real-time notifications and automatic emergency alerts could provide additional security and help caregivers respond more promptly whenever abnormal health conditions occur.
	Similarly, Chung et al. (2023) examined the acceptance of smartwatch technology among older adults, particularly its use for health and location tracking. The study emphasized the importance of understanding older adults' perceptions, usability experiences, and willingness to adopt wearable monitoring devices. This supports the findings of the present study, which obtained a grand mean of 4.25 (Strongly Agree), indicating that elderly respondents are generally willing to use wearable health-monitoring technology. Their positive responses suggest that smartwatches can be considered a suitable tool for supporting elderly health monitoring, improving their sense of security, and helping caregivers become more aware of their condition.
Table 4
MECHANISM OR TECHNIQUES OF THE PROPOSED SYSTEM (CAREGIVER)
	The analysis of table 4 shows that caregivers strongly agree with the proposed integration of artificial intelligence and wearable health-monitoring technology into the caregiving process, with a grand mean of 4.53 (Strongly Agree). The highest mean of 4.64 (Strongly Agree) indicates that caregivers believe an AI chatbot that provides guidance and support would be a helpful tool in their daily caregiving tasks. Meanwhile, the lowest mean of 4.48 (Strongly Agree) relates to the use of AI to analyze elderly patients' vital signs to detect health risks more accurately and quickly. This suggests that caregivers are highly receptive to integrating AI-powered features into elderly care, particularly in providing assistance, supporting decision-making, identifying potential health risks, and improving emergency response. These findings indicate a positive level of acceptance toward the proposed ElderCareAI system and its potential to make caregiving more efficient and responsive.
	In 2022, Seok et al. (2022) developed and evaluated the TouchCare system, which integrated wearable technology, artificial intelligence, and a mobile application to support the health monitoring and daily care of older adults. The system used a wearable watch and sensors to collect activity data, while its context-aware AI analyzed the information and provided appropriate interventions. These findings further support ElderCareAI's proposed features, particularly AI-powered health analysis, automated notifications, and chatbot assistance to improve patient monitoring and support caregiver decision-making.
Table 5
TOP/KEY FEATURES OF THE PROPOSED SYSTEM 
	The analysis of table 5 shows that caregivers strongly agree with the proposed features and functionalities of the ElderCareAI system, with a grand mean of 4.58 (Strongly Agree). The highest mean of 4.61 (Strongly Agree) is shared by three features: smartwatch-based stress level monitoring, skin temperature deviation monitoring, and automated health report generation. This indicates that caregivers recognize the importance of having comprehensive health information, identifying possible health concerns, and reducing the time spent on manual documentation. Meanwhile, the lowest mean of 4.51 (Strongly Agree) relates to fitness activity tracking, which suggests that caregivers also recognize the value of monitoring changes in elderly patients' physical activity and mobility. Overall, these findings indicate that caregivers highly accept the proposed system's features, particularly its ability to provide continuous health monitoring, AI-powered health analysis, early risk identification, automated notifications, and efficient patient documentation.
	Song et al. (2024) conducted a study on the use of a digital health-monitoring platform to support older adults and their community caregivers. Their platform combined wearable technology, a mobile application, and a chatbot to gather and monitor health-related information, such as heart rate variability, sleep quality, physical activity, and stress levels. It also provided daily health feedback that enabled caregivers to stay informed about changes in the older adults' health conditions. The study reported improvements in participants' depressive symptoms and sleep quality. These findings are consistent with the results of the present study, which obtained a grand mean of 4.58 (Strongly Agree), demonstrating caregivers' positive acceptance of wearable monitoring and automated health-related technologies. 
	The study further highlights the potential of ElderCareAI to improve elderly care through continuous health monitoring, AI-assisted health analysis, automated caregiver notifications, and digital health reports that support timely interventions and more efficient caregiving.
